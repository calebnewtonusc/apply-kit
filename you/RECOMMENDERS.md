# Recommenders

**`/recommenders` fills this in.** For fellowships and grad school these often decide
the application. For jobs they come late. For grants, letters of support are a
different artifact: they commit an organization, not a person.

## The dates that actually bind

The recommender's deadline is earlier than yours, and the ask has to go out well before
that. This table is the whole point of the file.

| Org | Your deadline | Their real deadline | Ask sent | Confirmed submitted |
| --- | ------------- | ------------------- | -------- | ------------------- |
|     |               |                     |          |                     |

Six to eight weeks of notice for grad school and major fellowships. Three weeks is a
tight minimum. Not confirmed submitted is not done, and portals do not always tell you.

## Who, and why them

The best letter comes from specificity, not seniority. Someone who watched you work
beats someone senior who knows your name.

| Person | Relationship | What they can speak to, specifically | Asked | Said yes |
| ------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------ | ----- | -------- |
|        |              |                                      |       |          |

## The packet sent to each

Sent in one message, never in pieces, never after they ask:

- [ ] Deadline and exactly how to submit, with the link
- [ ] Resume or CV
- [ ] The statement or essays, even in draft
- [ ] What this application is for and which criteria it scores against
- [ ] Two or three specific things you did with them, with dates and details
- [ ] Anything the program wants the letter to address
