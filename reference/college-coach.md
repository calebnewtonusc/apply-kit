# Coaching someone else's college app

The playbook from coaching a high school senior through a USC Iovine and Young Academy Early Action app in October 2026. Use it when you're helping someone else, not writing your own. The applicant writes every word. You do everything around the words.

## First hour

1. Read every doc they shared, whole, tabs included. Their outline, their brainstorm doc and their own notes usually already answer half of what you'd ask. On the first pass, two of the five essay questions were already answered in a doc that hadn't been opened yet.
2. Make a status table of every required piece (essays, short answers, videos, portfolio, activities, recommenders) with its real state. Count words with a tool.
3. Read the school's live requirements page and write down the date you checked it. Mark what's required and what's optional. At IYA the portfolio is optional and the intro video plus two essays are required, which decides what gets cut when time runs out.
4. Check which schools they're actually submitting. It's the most important question and it's easy to never ask. Verify every early deadline on each school's own site, plus the traps (Michigan added a binding ED on the same day as EA, and Stanford REA limits other early apps).

## The guide doc

One shared Google Doc, one section per open piece, each with four parts:

- Where it stands, read from their docs that day
- Worth knowing: what the prompt is really grading, the word budget per section, any rule they're breaking (like 4 picks when the prompt says 1 to 3), and any fact that has to be verified
- 3 to 5 questions about moments and facts ("What did your pastor say, word for word?"), never themes
- What to open: their own doc, their own lines, the real course list

Put the questions in blue so they can tell at a glance what's theirs to answer. Open the doc with a one-thing-a-day plan through the deadline, required pieces first. Tell them a rough draft is fine every day until the last two.

## Never

- Write a sentence, hook, title or caption for them. Interviewers quote their own materials back to them.
- Quote guessed hours as fact. Hand them a blank hours map and do the math on their numbers.
- Put therapy, family or dating material from their private docs into a doc meant for coaching.
- Let a school fact stand without a current source. Facts from pre-opening press, other schools' pages, and "Ignite Grants" that no search could find all came out.

## Worth doing

- Pull real course names from each concentration's own page, so they can name an exact class. Faculty interviewers ask for exactly that.
- If the coach got into the same program, read the coach's own accepted app for patterns (essay shape, portfolio fields, interview questions) and hand those over as patterns, never as lines.
- Check that the applicant actually accepted their repo and doc invites, and that nobody else got invited by accident.
- Edit Google Docs through the Docs API with formatting-only batches, and diff the text before and after. Never mix a text change into a batch of index-based deletes.

## Later

After a few of these it can become a product: a coach that runs this playbook for any applicant. Every fact gets sourced, every question is aimed at the applicant's own material, and the applicant writes every word.
