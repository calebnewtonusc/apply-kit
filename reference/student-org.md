# Student organizations

Clubs, campus orgs, pre-professional societies, Greek life, service groups, student
government, campus consulting and venture groups.

## Who reads it

Another student. Usually a sophomore or junior officer, on a couch, at 11pm, with
somewhere between 80 and 400 applications left. They are scoring against a rubric that
lives mostly in their head. Sixty to ninety seconds on yours unless something makes
them slow down.

They are not looking for the best applicant. They are looking for a reason to move you
to the yes pile so they can stop thinking about you.

The distinguishing feature of this type: **the reader will be your teammate.** No other
application type has that. They are deciding whether they want to sit next to you on
Tuesday nights for fifteen weeks, and that decision is made partly on the fun
questions, which is why the fun questions are real questions.

## The artifacts

- Written prompts, usually three to six, in a Google Form or Typeform
- A resume, often with a specific filename convention
- Sometimes a video, usually 60 to 90 seconds
- Sometimes a case or a mock deliverable for consulting and venture groups
- Two rounds is standard: written, then one or two interviews

## What is usually unasked

| Stated                       | Usually deciding                                                    |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Why our club                 | Why not the five others in this lane you are obviously also applying |
| A time you worked on a team  | Can you be told you are wrong                                        |
| What do you want to learn    | Are you here to learn or to be impressive                            |
| A social issue you care about| Will you still show up in week nine of an unglamorous project        |
| Favorite snack / song / show | Do we want to sit next to you every Tuesday                          |
| Reapplying? Tell us more     | What is actually different since we told you no                      |

## Format traps that cost people applications

- Filename conventions, often with a comma or underscore in a specific order
- One-page resume limits, sometimes stated twice on the same form
- Link permissions. Open every link in a private window. Clubs that warn twice about
  this do so because applicants keep failing it
- Word limits enforced by the form itself, which will silently truncate

## Timeline

Fast. Recruitment usually runs one to three weeks from info session to decision,
concentrated in the first month of a semester. Deadlines cluster, so the conflict
check in `/targets` matters more here than anywhere else: several orgs hold mandatory
meetings in the same block, you can accept exactly one, and you will be asked in an
interview which you would pick.

## What counts as a specific

Ranked: something you did with them (info session, talked to a member, used something
they built), a named project or client of theirs, a named person and what they said,
an alum outcome, a structural fact about how they run a semester.

Their mission statement is not a specific. Neither is any adjective from their
Instagram bio.

## The interview

Off the page in front of them, line by line. "Walk me through that number" is the
default follow-up. Consulting and venture groups run cases and expect you to ask
clarifying questions and say "let me take thirty seconds to structure this," which is
scored rather than penalized.

## AI policy

The one type where explicit bans are common, usually in an honor-code checkbox near
the top of the form. Check every form and set the field. See `AI-POLICY.md`.
