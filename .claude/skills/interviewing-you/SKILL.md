# Getting material out of a person

The bottleneck was never writing. It is that nobody ever asked this person the right
question about their own life. Most of the value in this kit is here.

## Rules

**Never ask what is already answered.** Read `you/PROFILE.md`, `you/STORY-BANK.md`,
`you/VOICE.md`, `you/uploads/`, and the relevant `applications/` file first. Asking
someone to repeat themselves is how these tools lose people.

**Three to five questions at a time.** Numbered. Never twenty.

**Say fragments are fine, explicitly.** They will write paragraphs otherwise, badly,
and then be tired. Tell them: fragments, bullet points, one line, voice-memo
transcripts, all fine. Prose is your job.

**Lead with the blocking question.** If one answer unblocks three applications, say
that out loud. "Answer number 2 and I can write 180DC, RISE, and BTG tonight."

**Ask for facts and opinions, not writing.** "What would you actually do with the
$10,000" gets you material. "Write about your community" gets you a bad paragraph you
then have to undo.

**Never ask permission to do work they already asked for.** Do it, then report.

## The pick-list move

This is the one that changes outcomes. When someone is tired, stuck, or facing a
question about themselves they have never articulated, **stop asking open questions
and give them multiple choice built from their own material.**

```markdown
## 12. Your life as a TV show (BTG, 50 words)

**12a. Genre**

- **A.** Sports drama that turns into a tech drama.
- **B.** Coming-of-age comedy with an unreasonably high-stakes B plot.
- **C.** Docuseries where the guy keeps announcing a new plan and the narrator
  gets tired.

**12b. Season one cliffhanger**

- **A.** She finally declares a major. Cut to black before she says which one.
- **B.** The app gets approved. The phone buzzes. She is asleep.
```

They answer "A, C" and you have a real answer. Four minutes of their time.

Rules for pick lists:

- Every option must come from something they actually told you. No inventing a life.
- Always end with "none of these, here is mine."
- Make the options genuinely different, not three shades of the same answer.
- It is fine for one option to be funny. It is not fine for all of them to be safe.
- Number them continuously across the whole file so they can answer "14a, 15c, 16b" in
  one line.

## Intake specifically

You are building a story bank, not a form. Go in this order:

1. **Inventory.** What have you done. Fast, listy, no depth yet. Ten minutes.
2. **Pick the three that have a scene in them.** A scene means a moment with a time,
   a place, and something at stake. Most people's best material is the thing they
   almost did not mention.
3. **Dig on those three.** What went wrong. What did it cost. What did you do at the
   worst point. What number can you put on it. Who else was there.
4. **The thesis question.** What do you believe that people around you do not? This is
   the highest-yield question in intake and most people have never been asked it.
5. **The reversal question.** What did you believe two years ago that you no longer
   believe? Half of all application prompts are secretly this question.
6. **Numbers pass.** For every claim, get the defensible figure and where it comes
   from. Write it in PROFILE.md once. It goes in every document unchanged.
7. **The boundaries pass.** Is there anything you do not want in any application? Write
   it in `you/DISCLOSURE.md` and honor it without relitigating.

## Digging

When an answer is thin, the follow-ups that work:

- "What was the worst moment of that?"
- "What did you actually do, hour by hour?"
- "What did it cost you?"
- "Who else was in the room?"
- "What would have happened if you had done nothing?"
- "What is the part of that story you usually leave out?"

The last one is the best question in the list. Use it once per session, not more.

## Reading their uploads

If they give you a resume, past essays, or writing samples, mine them before asking
anything. Resumes especially: every bullet is a story compressed to one line, and
"what happened behind this bullet" is a better prompt than anything you would invent.
