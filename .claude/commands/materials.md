Videos, portfolios, writing samples, work samples, decks, take-homes, transcripts.
Read the org's `applications/` file, load `application-types`, and read the matching
file in `reference/`.

These lose applications quietly. A strong essay packet with a broken portfolio link, a
video forty seconds over, or a writing sample outside the page range fails on the
mechanics, and nobody writes back to tell you which one it was.

Write findings into the org's `applications/` file under **Format requirements**, and
put anything with lead time into `DEADLINES.md`.

## First, the inventory

List every non-prose artifact this org requires, copied from the form: what it is, the
exact spec, whether it is required or optional, and whether it exists yet. Optional
artifacts count. Declining an optional invitation answers a question badly, especially
when reapplying.

## Video

Most-watched artifact and the least prepared.

- Length limit, enforced by the upload. Time the actual file, do not estimate.
- Whether they ask for unedited or unscripted. Some programs say so and mean it, and an
  over-produced video reads worse than a plain one.
- Say what the thing is in the first ten seconds. Introductions and thanks come after.
- Show the thing if it exists.
- Format and hosting, and whether the link is public. Check it in a private window.

Prep is a bulleted outline, not a script. A read script is visible immediately.

## Portfolio, GitHub, work samples

- Open every link in a private window. This is the single most common silent failure in
  the whole kit.
- Pin or feature the two or three things that match what this org does, and make sure
  the landing page is not a wall of unlabeled work.
- Each piece needs one line saying what it is, what your role was, and what happened.
  Work that requires the viewer to reconstruct the context does not get looked at.
- If any of it was team work, say what you did. A reviewer who cannot tell reads the
  whole thing as ambiguous.
- Nothing behind a login, nothing needing an access request.

## Writing sample

For `grad-school` and some `fellowship` and `job` applications.

- Page or word range, honored exactly.
- Pick for the kind of thinking the program cares about rather than for the grade it
  got. An excerpt from a longer piece is usually allowed and often better, with a
  one-paragraph note saying what it is from.
- Clean it up. An old paper with a professor's comments still in the margins gets sent
  more often than anyone would guess.

## Take-homes and cases

- Time limit, and whether it is enforced by the platform.
- Do the stated task before the interesting version of it. The most common failure is
  building something better than what was asked and missing a requirement.
- Say your assumptions in writing. Reviewers score reasoning, not just output.
- Submit the working thing rather than the ambitious broken thing.

## Transcripts and institutional documents

These are the ones with real lead time. Official transcripts take days and sometimes
cost money. Registration and eligibility steps in a grant or fellowship portal can take
longer than that. They go into `DEADLINES.md` under things with lead time, dated
backward from the deadline, the moment you learn they are required.

## Report

A checklist per artifact: spec, status, and what to do next. Then the one that is most
likely to fail and what it would cost. Anything blocked on another person or on an
institution gets called out separately, because those cannot be fixed the night before.
