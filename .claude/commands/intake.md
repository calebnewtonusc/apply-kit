---
description: Interview the applicant and build their story bank
---

Load the `interviewing-you` skill and follow it.

**Before asking anything:** read everything in `you/uploads/`, plus `you/PROFILE.md`
and `you/STORY-BANK.md` in case a previous session got partway. Mine the uploads
hard. A resume is a stack of stories compressed to one line each, and "what happened
behind this bullet" beats any question you would invent.

If `you/uploads/` is empty, ask for a resume or a LinkedIn export first. Say it will
cut the interview roughly in half. Then proceed without it if they do not have one.

## Run it in seven passes

Three to five questions per pass. Tell them fragments are fine. Never ask something
the uploads already answered.

1. **Inventory.** What they have done. Fast, listy, no depth.
2. **Scene-finding.** Identify the three items that have an actual moment in them: a
   time, a place, something at stake. Say which three you picked and why.
3. **Digging** on those three. What went wrong, what it cost, what they did at the
   worst point, who else was there, what number fits.
4. **The thesis question.** What do you believe that people around you do not? Highest
   yield question in the whole kit. Push for a claim someone could disagree with, not
   a value.
5. **The reversal question.** What did you believe two years ago that you no longer
   believe? Half of all prompts are secretly this.
6. **Numbers.** For every claim, the defensible figure and what it counts. Watch for
   the same thing counted two ways.
7. **Boundaries.** Anything they do not want in any application, for any reason. No
   follow-up questions, no persuading.

## Then write three files

**`you/PROFILE.md`**: the facts. Name, year, school, major, roles with real dates,
skills they would happily be quizzed on, commitments and weekly hours, and a
**Canonical numbers** section that every other document must match.

**`you/STORY-BANK.md`**: the material, organized as:

- **The lead.** Their single strongest thing, stated as a contestable claim.
- **Five stories, prepared cold**, per the `mock-interview` skill: something shipped,
  something handed off or lost, a collapse handled, a person helped, a time they were
  wrong. Each with the specifics.
- **Threads.** Recurring subjects that can angle toward any org.
- **Dead lines.** Anything true once and false now, or true but off-limits. Quote the
  exact sentences so no future session reuses them.

**`you/DISCLOSURE.md`**: what they will and will not say, with their reasoning in
their words, and any per-org exceptions.

Then tell them what they now have, name the strongest piece of material in the bank
and why, and point them at `/voice`.
