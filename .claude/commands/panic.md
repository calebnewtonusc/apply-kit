Something is due soon and it is not ready. Do not open with reassurance and do not
lecture about planning. Both waste minutes that matter.

## First, sixty seconds of facts

Read the org's `applications/` file and its draft. Run `tools/count.sh` on it. Establish
four things and state them in four lines:

1. Real deadline and time zone, and how long is actually left.
2. What exists: which questions are drafted, which are empty.
3. What is blocking: open `[NEED:]` markers, and whether anything depends on another
   person, which cannot be fixed in the time left.
4. Whether the format requirements are met, since a perfect application in the wrong
   file type fails anyway.

## Then triage by what is left

**More than a day.** This is not a panic, it is a plan. Run the `GAPS.md` Section A
questions, draft, and skip straight to `/submit` afterward. Say that.

**A few hours.** Triage hard, and say what you are dropping.

- Answer every required question at any quality before improving any of them. An
  unanswered required field is a zero. A mediocre answer is not.
- Fill `[NEED:]` markers by asking all of them at once, numbered, in one message. Not
  three at a time. This is the one moment the usual pacing rule is wrong.
- Anything that cannot be sourced in time comes out of the answer rather than getting
  invented. Rewrite the sentence to be true without the missing fact. **The one rule
  does not bend under time pressure**, and this is exactly when people break it.
- Get every answer inside its limit with `tools/count.sh`. Over the limit can be a hard
  fail in the form. Under 90 percent is a cost you accept tonight.
- Skip `/slop` and `/rank`. Run the format checks and the numbers check, which are the
  two that fail applications outright.

**Under an hour.** Required fields, exact limits, correct file type and filename, links
opened in a private window, submit. Nothing else. Say plainly that this is what you are
doing and why.

## What to say about quality

Be honest and brief. If the application going in is weaker than it should be, say so
once, without softening and without piling on. They know. Then keep working.

If it genuinely cannot be finished, say that early rather than at minute forty. Some
orgs accept late, some have a second round, some have a next cycle. Those are real
options and finding out takes two minutes.

## Afterward, in one line

Point at `/plan` for the next one, and at the **Next cycle, earlier** section that
`/outcome` writes. Then drop it. A retrospective the night of a deadline is not useful
and they are not in a state to hear it.
