Build the reapplication for **$1**. Read that org's `applications/` file, `you/PROVEN.md`
if it exists, and anything in `you/uploads/` from the earlier attempt.

Reapplying is the strongest position in this repo and almost nobody treats it that way.
They already looked at this person and did not say never. The whole application turns
on one question that most forms do not ask out loud.

## The question under the whole thing

**What is actually different now.**

Not "I want it more." Not "I have grown." Different means a thing that exists now and
did not exist then: a project shipped, a skill used on something real, a role held, a
gap closed, a semester of evidence. If nothing is different, the honest move is to say
that plainly to the applicant before drafting, because a reapplication that repeats the
first one confirms the original decision.

Ask them directly, once: **what have you done since they said no?** Then find the one
thing in the answer that a reader could verify.

## First, get the diagnosis right

In order of how much it is worth:

1. **Real feedback, if it exists.** Many orgs give it on request, and grant funders
   almost always do. If they have it, it outranks every guess. If they never asked and
   the window is still open, tell them to ask.
2. **Where they got cut.** Written round or interview. These are different problems and
   the fix is not the same. Losing at the interview means the written material works,
   and more drafting is the wrong response. Route to `/interview` instead.
3. **The old answers**, if they still have them. Run `/proven` on any that got them to
   a later round, and read the ones that did not for what a stranger would notice now.

Ask, take the answer, move on. Do not spend the session speculating about why.

## What changes in the draft

**Say it once, early, without apology.** One or two flat sentences naming the
reapplication and what changed. Buried, it reads as hoping they will not notice.
Apologized for, it reads as someone still carrying the rejection. Rehearsed to boring
is the target, same as any hard sentence in `you/DISCLOSURE.md`.

**Take every optional field.** Declining an optional invitation while reapplying
answers the real question badly, and almost nobody takes it, which is exactly why it
works.

**Do not reuse an answer they already rejected.** Even a good one. The reader may not
remember, but the point is that the new material is the argument.

**Update the specifics.** A year has passed. Officers rotated, projects ended, funding
priorities moved. Run `/whyus` fresh rather than trusting last cycle's file, and load
the `org-research` skill for the parts that go stale.

## Then

Write the difference into the org's `applications/` file under **The angle**, so every
answer in the draft is working the same argument. Tell the applicant, in one sentence,
what that argument is. If you cannot state it in one sentence, the reapplication does
not have one yet, and that is the thing to fix before drafting.
