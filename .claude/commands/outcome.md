Record the result for $1. This is the command that makes the kit compound.

Everything else here works from general principles about what applications reward.
Outcomes are the only evidence about what **this applicant's** material actually does
in front of real readers. Most people throw it away, apply again next cycle, and repeat
the same mistakes with fresh enthusiasm.

## Write it into `you/OUTCOMES.md`

Per application:

- Org, type, cycle, date of the result
- The result, exactly. Interview, offer, rejected after written, rejected after
  interview, waitlisted, never heard back
- **Which round it ended in.** The most useful field in the file, because it separates
  a writing problem from an interview problem
- Any feedback given, verbatim, including the throwaway line in a rejection email
- What the applicant thinks happened, in their words, asked once

Then, from the file as a whole, look for what one application cannot show:

- **Cut at the written round repeatedly** means the material is not landing, and the
  next cycle starts at `/intake` rather than `/draft`.
- **Passing written and losing interviews** means more drafting is wasted effort. That
  cycle is `/interview`, and it is a different kind of work.
- **One type doing much worse than another.** Read that type's file in `reference/`
  again and check whether the applications were actually written for that reader.
- **Never hearing back at all**, especially for `job`, which usually points at the
  resume or at applying past a real closing date rather than at the essays.

Say what the pattern is, in two sentences, or say plainly that there are not enough
results yet to see one. Three applications is not a pattern.

## Then close the loops

**If anything got to a later round, run `/proven` on those answers.** Structural moves
that cleared a real round are worth more than any general advice in this repo, and this
is the only moment they get captured while the outcome is known.

**Update `you/PROFILE.md`** with anything the cycle changed: a new role, a completed
project, a number that moved.

**Mark dead lines** in `you/STORY-BANK.md`. An answer that failed twice in the same
form is worth quoting there so no future session reaches for it.

**Note anything worth doing earlier next time**, with the date it should happen. The
recommender ask that went out late, the info session that was already over, the posting
that closed in September. These go in `you/OUTCOMES.md` under **Next cycle, earlier**,
and `/plan` reads them.

## On a rejection

Say it plainly, record it, and move on to the work. Do not editorialize about fit or
their loss. If they want to talk about it they will, and the useful thing you can offer
is the diagnosis of which round it ended in, which is a fact rather than a comfort.

If the org gives feedback on request and the window is open, say so. That is the single
highest-value action available after a no, and most people do not know it is on offer.
