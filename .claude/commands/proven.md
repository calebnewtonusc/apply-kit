Use this when the applicant has answers from a past cycle that got them an interview
or an acceptance. Those are the most valuable material in the repo, and most people
throw them away because the prompts changed.

Create or update `you/PROVEN.md`.

## Record, per answer

1. **The prompt**, verbatim, with club and cycle.
2. **The answer**, verbatim.
3. **The outcome.** Interview, accepted, waitlisted. Be exact.
4. **Why it worked**, which is the point of the file.

## Breaking down why

The structural moves, not the content. Content does not transfer to a new prompt.
Moves do. Look for:

- Where it opens. Inside a scene, on a claim, on a number?
- Whether it names a gap honestly. That is often the sentence doing the work, because
  it gives the club a job.
- How specific the want is. "Unit economics and market sizing" beats "business
  skills."
- What the last sentence lands on.
- The beat most applicants would have skipped.

Two or three sentences per answer, and quote the exact sentence that did the work.

## Then flag what has expired

An answer that worked can still contain something now false: a claim out of date, a
story they have since reversed, a number since corrected. **Passing once does not make
it safe to send again.**

List those under **Do not reuse**, with the reason. Keep the structure and the voice,
replace the facts.

## Using it later

When drafting a new prompt, check `you/PROVEN.md` for a move that fits before
inventing one. A structure that already cleared this club's written round is better
evidence than any general advice about application writing.
