Find the specific things about **$1** that make a "why us" answer unfakeable, and
write them into that org's `applications/` file under a **Specifics** heading.

Every applicant writes "your commitment to real client work resonates with me." The
org has read it forty times tonight. One sentence naming a project they actually ran
beats a paragraph of their own website adjectives handed back to them.

## What counts as a specific

Ranked by how much it is worth, best first.

1. **Something you did with them.** Went to the info session, talked to a member,
   attended a workshop, used something they built. Nobody who skipped it can write
   this. If `you/CHATS.md` has a conversation logged for this org, that is the best
   material in the file and it goes first.
2. **A named project or client**, with what they actually did and what came of it.
3. **A named person and something they said**, sourced. An officer's talk, a panel, a
   post.
4. **An alum outcome**, where they went and what they did there.
5. **A structural fact** about how the org works: a case competition they run, a
   curriculum, a semester-long project cycle, a partner org.
6. **A number** they publish about themselves.

## What does not count

Their mission statement. Their values page. Adjectives from their Instagram bio.
"Selective." "Driven community." Anything that would be true of nine other orgs in
the same lane on the same campus. If you cannot tell which org a sentence is about
with the name removed, it is not a specific.

## The rule while researching

**Never invent a fact about the org.** A hallucinated project name in a "why us"
answer gets caught by the one person in the room who was on that project, and it is
worse than a generic answer because it looks like a lie rather than laziness.

Load the `org-research` skill and follow it. Every specific you write down carries
its source URL and the date you checked. Anything you could not verify goes under
**Unverified, do not use** with what to check.

## Then, the harder half

Research gets you what is true about them. The answer also needs what is true about
the applicant, so finish with the connection:

- **Which specific fits which of their stories**, named, from `you/STORY-BANK.md`.
- **What this org has that the others in its cluster do not.** Read the other
  `applications/` files in the cluster. If your specific would work for two of them,
  it is not specific enough yet.
- **What the applicant would do for this org**, in their terms. Half the "why us"
  answers in a packet should run this direction rather than "teach me," and this is
  where that sentence gets built.

## If you come up empty

Say so plainly rather than filling the section with mush. Then tell them the two
things that would fix it fastest: go to the info session on the date in
`DEADLINES.md`, or message one member and run `/chat`. Thirty minutes of that beats
any amount of searching, and it is the only way to get a tier-one specific.
