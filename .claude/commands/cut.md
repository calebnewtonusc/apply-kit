Cut $1 to its limit. Run `tools/count.sh` for the count before and after and report
what it says. Do not estimate a word count.

## Order of cuts

Take from the top of this list first. Stop as soon as you are under.

1. **The closing reflection.** "What it changed about how I think." Everyone writes it
   and it reads most like everyone.
2. **Adverbs and hedges.** Really, very, quite, somewhat, I think, kind of, sort of.
3. **Setup before the first concrete detail.** Most answers actually start in their
   second or third sentence. Say so when that is true. It is the most useful thing
   this command finds.
4. **Repeated context** the reader already got from another answer on the same form.
5. **The second example.** If two things illustrate one point, keep the better one.
6. **Explanations of the obvious.** If the story shows it, do not also state it.

## Never cut

- A number, a name, or a mechanism. These are the whole value.
- The specific detail that makes it theirs.
- The part that answers the unasked question.

## When it will not fit

Say so. A story that needs setup you do not have room for is the wrong story for that
limit, and the fix is a different story, not tighter sentences. This is most true when
a hard subject gets raised with no room to resolve it: the reader gets the weight and
none of the landing.

Offer a specific alternative from `you/STORY-BANK.md` that fits the limit.

## Report

Before and after counts, the new version, and one line on what was lost. If something
real was lost, say it rather than pretending the short version is strictly better.
