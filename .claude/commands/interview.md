Load `mock-interview` and follow it.

## First, once

If they have interviewed with any of these clubs before and not gotten in, ask what
actually happened in the room: ran long, froze, got a case they had not seen, talked
past the question, could not recall a number. Each has a different drill, and prepping
against a guess wastes their week.

Ask it once, take the answer, move on.

## Build the question bank from their own page

Before any behavioral questions, go down the resume and the drafts and pull every
number and claim. For each, ask "how did you get that" and time the answer. Anything
they cannot walk through in ninety seconds goes on the drill list or comes off the
page. This is the highest-value part of interview prep and almost nobody does it.

## Then run the mock

- One question at a time. Wait for the full answer. Do not coach mid-answer.
- **Always ask the follow-up.** That is where interviews are lost. "What would you
  have done differently?" "Who disagreed with you?" "What happened to it after you
  left?" "How did you get that number?"
- Ask the question they are dreading. Once.
- Time each answer. Over two minutes on a behavioral question is a problem worth
  naming.
- After each: three lines only. What landed, what a real interviewer pushes on, one
  specific change. Then next question. No lectures.

## Cover

- The five stories from `you/STORY-BANK.md`, each asked from an angle they did not
  expect
- Anything sensitive in their history, rehearsed to two flat sentences and a return to
  the present, respecting `you/DISCLOSURE.md`
- "If all of these clubs said yes, which would you pick?" They will be asked, and if
  several have conflicting mandatory meetings they will definitely be asked
- The club's published rubric, if it has one, mapped to one story each

## End with

Write `review/INTERVIEW-PREP.md`: the five stories in one line each, the numbers they
need cold, the two answers that need rehearsing out loud, and the single question they
are least ready for.
