---
description: Ask only the questions that are actually blocking a draft
---

Load the `interviewing-you` skill.

Read every file in `applications/`, all of `you/`, and anything in `drafts/`. Work out
what you genuinely cannot write without them, and ask **only that**.

Write `GAPS.md` and tell them it is there.

## Structure it by what it unblocks, not by club

Open with the deadline math: which questions unblock which applications, and what is
due first. Something like "Section A alone unblocks the three due Friday."

- **Section A: blocking for the nearest deadline.** Nothing else goes here.
- **Section B: blocking, but later.**
- **Section C: would improve a draft, not blocking.** Explicitly optional. Say so.
- **Section D: cannot be drafted regardless.** Clubs with AI bans, applications that
  need a decision from someone else, anything needing a document only they can make.
  Say what you can still do for those.

## How to ask

- Numbered continuously so they can answer "3, 7, 9" in one line.
- Fragments are fine. Say it at the top of the file.
- Facts and opinions, not prose. "What would you actually do with the $10,000" not
  "write about your community."
- Where a question is really a decision rather than a fact, name it as a decision and
  give the tradeoff in two lines. Do not decide for them.
- If a question is uncomfortable, ask it plainly and once.

## Cap it

If the list runs past about fifteen questions, you are asking for things you could
infer. Cut to the ones that actually block a sentence, and convert the softer ones
into `/picks` format instead, where answering is four minutes of letters.

End by telling them: answer Section A and you can draft tonight.
