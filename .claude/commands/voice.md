---
description: Read the applicant's existing writing so drafts sound like them
---

Read everything in `you/uploads/` that they wrote themselves: old essays, personal
statements, past applications, cover letters, long messages, blog posts, anything.

If there is nothing, ask for two samples and say what makes a good one: something they
wrote for a person rather than for a form, and something they wrote fast. Then wait.

## What to extract

Write `you/VOICE.md` with these sections, each with **quoted examples from their
actual writing**. Never describe a pattern without showing it.

**Sentence rhythm.** Average length, and where they go short. Most people have one
move: a long setup then a four-word sentence, or the reverse. Name theirs.

**How they open.** Scene, claim, question, number, joke? Look at five openings and
find the pattern. This is the highest-value section.

**How they close.** Most people either land on a concrete detail or reach for a
lesson. If they reach for the lesson, say so, because that is the habit most worth
breaking.

**Vocabulary.** Words they actually use. Words they never use. Register: do they say
"kids" or "students," "built" or "developed," "got" or "obtained."

**Punctuation habits.** Comma splices, sentence fragments, parentheticals, dashes.
Keep the deliberate ones. They are the fingerprint.

**Humor.** Do they joke, and how? Dry, self-deprecating, absurd, none? An answer
written funny by someone who is not is the most obvious tell in an application.

**Tics to lose.** Their own crutches, quoted. Everyone has three. Usually a repeated
transition, a hedge like "kind of" or "I guess," or one overused adjective.

**Three sentences they wrote that no one else would have written.** These are the
target. Every draft is trying to sound like these.

## Then

Tell them the two or three patterns you will imitate, and the two you will avoid, in
plain language. If their writing already has AI slop patterns in it from a previous
tool, say so directly and do not adopt those.

Point them at `/targets` next.
